Unlike most existing literature, which treats radioisotope applications and green chemistry as separate topics, this study deliberately synthesizes both fields into a single risk-benefit framework, showing how the same underlying principle—engineered risk reduction—applies across nuclear science (shorter half-lives, containment systems) and green chemistry (waste prevention, safer chemical design). Rather than simply cataloguing uses the way regulatory sources like the NRC or EPA do, the study is comparative: it weighs benefits against risks across multiple STEM fields (medicine, engineering, applied sciences) at once and explicitly links nuclear science and green chemistry as complementary tools for sustainability, a pairing not commonly framed together in the individual sources reviewed. Since this is a literature synthesis rather than original experimental research, its originality lies not in new data but in how it organizes and connects existing institutional, industrial, and academic sources into one coherent analysis.
